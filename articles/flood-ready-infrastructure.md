# Flood-Ready Infrastructure Starts with Better Data

Flood resilience begins with understanding where water goes today and how that pattern could change. A single design storm is rarely enough to describe a city's real exposure.

## Combine different evidence

Use rainfall records, terrain surveys, drainage plans, asset condition reports, and reports of past flooding. Each source has limits: a map may miss a blocked inlet, while an anecdote may miss the wider catchment. Together they provide a better basis for decisions.

## Test several scenarios

Assess frequent storms that disrupt daily life as well as rarer events that threaten critical services. Consider the effect of new development and potential climate changes. The purpose is to identify failure paths, not just calculate a single water level.

## Prioritize useful upgrades

Some problems can be reduced through maintenance or small changes to inlet capacity. Others need storage, changed drainage routes, raised equipment, or redesigned crossings. Compare options by consequence, cost, and the ability to phase the work.

## Keep the model current

Record actual flood events and maintenance findings after each season. Updating the asset data makes the next investment decision more reliable.
