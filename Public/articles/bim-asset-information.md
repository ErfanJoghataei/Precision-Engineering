# From BIM Model to Useful Asset Information

A detailed building information model is not automatically a useful handover. Owners need the right information to operate, inspect, repair, and replace assets long after construction ends.

## Start with owner questions

Which equipment needs planned maintenance? What needs statutory inspection? Which components are difficult to replace? The answers determine what information belongs in the handover.

## Use consistent identifiers

Give each maintainable asset a stable ID that appears in the model, schedules, drawings, and maintenance system. Record its location, manufacturer, warranty, and key performance data. Consistency matters more than the number of fields.

## Make revisions traceable

Construction changes should flow into the final model and documents. Record revision dates and the source of each important change so operators can trust what they receive.

## Test the handover with a real task

Before delivery, ask a facilities team member to locate a component, find its maintenance instructions, and identify how to isolate it safely. If that takes too long, the handover needs work even if the model looks complete.
